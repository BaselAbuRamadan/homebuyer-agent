# Buying a new-construction home: the typical process

Buying from a builder differs from buying a resale home. Most purchases follow these stages:

1. **Get pre-approved.** A lender reviews income, debts and credit and states how much they would lend. Builders often ask for a pre-approval letter before you sign a contract.
2. **Choose a community and home type.** Homes are usually sold as *move-in ready* (finished), *under construction* (some choices already made), or *to be built* (you pick a lot and floor plan; typically 6–12 months to complete).
3. **Sign the purchase agreement.** Builder contracts are written by the builder and favour the builder. Having your own buyer's agent or a real-estate attorney review it is recommended.
4. **Pay the earnest money deposit.** Often a percentage of the price; terms on refunds vary by contract.
5. **Design centre selections** (to-be-built homes). Upgrades can add significantly to the final price, so set an upgrade budget first.
6. **Construction and inspections.** Even new homes benefit from independent inspections (pre-drywall and final).
7. **Final walkthrough** with the builder to create a "punch list" of items to fix.
8. **Closing.** Sign loan documents, pay closing costs, receive keys.

A buyer's agent is usually paid through the transaction, but you often must register them on your first visit to a community, so bring them (or name them) from the start.
