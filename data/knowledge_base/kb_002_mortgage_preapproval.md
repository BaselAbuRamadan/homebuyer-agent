# Mortgage pre-qualification vs pre-approval

**Pre-qualification** is a quick estimate based on information you report yourself. It is not verified.

**Pre-approval** means the lender has checked documents (pay slips, tax returns, bank statements) and run a credit check. It carries more weight with sellers and builders.

Lenders look at:
- **Debt-to-income ratio (DTI):** total monthly debt payments (including the new mortgage) divided by gross monthly income. Many conventional loans prefer a DTI at or below roughly 36–43%, though limits vary by loan programme.
- **Credit score:** affects whether you qualify and your interest rate.
- **Down payment:** conventional loans can allow as little as 3–5% down; below 20% usually adds private mortgage insurance (PMI). FHA loans allow lower down payments with mortgage insurance. VA loans may allow 0% down for eligible veterans.

A common rule of thumb is to keep total housing costs (principal, interest, taxes, insurance, HOA — "PITI + HOA") around 28% of gross monthly income. This is a guideline, not a rule.

Pre-approvals usually expire after 60–90 days.
